# Take-home: Plan, Gate, Build in practice

**Time:** about 1–2 hours, in your learning time, over the next 2 weeks
**Bring to:** your next 1:1 with your buddy or team lead
**Why bother?** Every part below produces **evidence for your competencies** — the table at the end tells you which ones and gives you a sentence template.

> Same rule as the session: nothing from work goes into a public AI tool. No customer or member data, no secrets, no internal code. If you're not sure whether a tool is approved for something, ask your team lead first.

---

## Part 1 — Same ticket, different route (30–45 min)

Run [JR-102](TICKET.md) again on your own, changing **one** thing:

- a **different AI tool** from the one your team used, or
- a **different kind of output** (if you built an app, write the document or design the API).

Keep the four steps: spar for a plan, human gate, build, review. Save both plans — the AI's and yours.

**Developer track**

1. Get the build running or readable end to end, and fix anything the AI got wrong by hand.
2. Write down three things you changed in the AI's output and why.

**QE track**

1. Before the build, write three to five acceptance checks from the agreed plan.
2. Run the build against them and note what passed, what failed and what the plan never covered.

---

## Part 2 — Try it on something real (30–45 min)

Pick a small task from your own work or learning where AI is allowed — a script, a test plan, a piece of documentation, a learning exercise.

1. Write the planning prompt first. Make the AI ask you questions before it plans.
2. Do the human gate properly: note at least one thing you changed or cut.
3. Build, then review it against the plan.

If your squad uses Navigator, try the same task with its workflow skills and compare how it handles the planning step.

---

## Part 3 — Your own pros and cons (15 min)

Write half a page, in your own words:

- Two things AI did well for you, with an example of each.
- Two things it did badly or got wrong, with an example of each.
- One rule you'll follow from now on when you use it.

---

## Part 4 — Log your competency evidence (15 min)

Copy the rows that fit what you actually did into your competencies sheet. Mapped to the
[Government Digital and Data Profession Capability Framework](https://ddat-capability-framework.service.gov.uk/) —
if your sheet uses different headings, match on the description.

### Developers — Junior developer

| Skill (level expected)                     | What in this exercise shows it                                  | Framework wording it matches                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Programming and build** (Working)        | Reviewing and correcting an AI-generated build against a plan   | "design, code, test, correct and document simple programs or scripts under the direction of others" |
| **Modern development standards** (Working) | Plan before build, a human review gate, checking output         | "apply modern development standards principles under guidance"                                      |
| **Systems design** (Awareness)             | Comparing design options and trade-offs before choosing a plan  | "assist as part of a team in the design of components of larger systems"                            |
| **User focus** (Awareness)                 | Designing for a new starter; deciding what "done" means for them | "show an awareness or understanding of user experience analysis and its principles"                 |

### QEs — Quality assurance test analyst

| Skill                                                     | What in this exercise shows it                               | Framework wording it matches                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Test analysis**                                         | Spotting assumptions and gaps in the AI's plan at the gate   | "explain what might be a risk in achieving quality goals"                                |
| **Designing and executing tests**                         | Acceptance checks written from the plan, run against the build | "design, build and maintain simple tests that align to user needs and requirements"      |
| **Communicating between the technical and non-technical** | Part 3 — pros and cons in plain English                      | "explain why it's important to communicate technical concepts in non-technical language" |

### Evidence sentence template

> **Situation:** In the Jr Plan, Gate, Build session / on [task], [one line on what needed doing].
> **What I did:** I [wrote the planning prompt / challenged the plan at the gate / reviewed the build and fixed…].
> **Result:** [what was produced, what I changed from the AI's version, who I shared it with].
> **What I learned:** [one line].

Keep it to 3–4 lines per competency. A link to your plans, your build or your write-up is worth more than a paragraph.
